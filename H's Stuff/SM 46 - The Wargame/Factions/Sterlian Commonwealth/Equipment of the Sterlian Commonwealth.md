# Calculation Equipment
**Mk.III “Kafka” Artillery Computer**  
The Mark III Centralized Artillery Fire Control Computer (CAFCC, pronounced “Kaf-ka”) was an advanced mechanical computer developed in the last years of the Great War. The system is an evolution of similar machines used by the Royal Navy, modified to fit the relatively small size of a map table. Its onboard calculators could provide accurate firing solutions for up to 16 batteries and 32 targets at once. This provided controllers with a top-down view of the battlefield, decreasing response times and friendly fire incidents. First deployed to Sterlian frontline units in mid-1924, a single machine was credited for winning the artillery duel at the Second Battle of Saint-Chamond-sur-Marne, and the model as a whole for the wider success of the 1924 Summer Offensive.
